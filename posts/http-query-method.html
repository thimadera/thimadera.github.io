For years, HTTP gave us two options for reading data, and neither was quite right.

GET is the honest one: safe, idempotent, cacheable. But everything has to fit in the URL. Complex filters, nested objects, arrays of IDs… the URL gets huge, hard to read, and eventually hits length limits.

And yes, a lot of people just send a GET with a body. It might even work on your server, but that's not what GET was made for: the spec says a GET body has no defined meaning, and proxies, CDNs and HTTP clients may drop it or reject the request. In the browser, fetch() won't even let you do it.

POST fixes that with a request body. But now your read looks like a write: caches won't store it, clients won't retry it automatically, and nobody can tell from the method whether it changes anything.

Meet QUERY, now officially standardized as RFC 10008.

QUERY /products/search
Content-Type: application/json

{ "category": "shoes", "sizes": [40, 41], "maxPrice": 300 }

It's the best of both worlds:
→ a request body, like POST
→ safe: it doesn't change server state, like GET
→ idempotent: safe to retry after a network failure, like GET
→ cacheable: the body becomes part of the cache key, like GET

Servers can even advertise support with the Accept-Query header.

Frameworks, proxies and CDNs still need to catch up, but the method is official. That means POST /search is on its way out.
